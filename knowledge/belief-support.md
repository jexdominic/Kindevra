# Responding to unverified experiences

Reviewed against primary public health guidance on 2026-10-22; clinical review pending.

## Provenance and intended behavior

- [NSW Health: communicating with someone experiencing psychosis](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/mentalhealth/psychosocial/strategies/Pages/communicating-psychosis.aspx) supports calm, respectful communication focused on the person's feelings without arguing about beliefs. Source register: `nsw-psychosis-communication`.
- [NIMH: Understanding Psychosis](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/understanding-psychosis) explains that assessment belongs with qualified professionals and encourages seeking care for persistent or increasing difficulties. Source register: `nimh-psychosis`.

Kindevra's engineering rule is to acknowledge uncertainty, ask how the experience feels, and offer professional and trusted-person support. Do not confirm the belief, declare it false, diagnose the person, promise safety, encourage investigation or confrontation, or provide an automatic thought exercise in place of this support. An unusual belief alone does not establish an emergency. Existing explicit danger routes retain priority.

This is an original product response informed by these sources, not a copied clinical protocol or validated intervention. The source register contains links and short summaries only. No model training, full-text ingestion or clinical validation has occurred.

## Scope and limitations

The route recognizes a few English patterns about implanted devices, personal broadcast messages and thought access. It deliberately excludes some negations, fiction, quotations and metaphors within a sentence. It cannot distinguish every real event, medical intervention, cultural belief, metaphor or report about another person, and can miss unusual wording. Its output must never be interpreted as psychosis detection. The rule does not retain a belief or diagnosis in session state.

General crisis negation remains unresolved. Broader language understanding, mixed intentions, qualified clinical review and browser verification remain necessary.
